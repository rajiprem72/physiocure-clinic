Arthritis Treatment – Symptoms, Causes, Physiotherapy & Joint Care

Discover how physiotherapy can reduce arthritis pain, improve joint mobility, strengthen muscles, and help you lead an active, independent life.

What is Arthritis?

Arthritis is a condition that causes inflammation of one or more joints, leading to pain, stiffness, swelling, and reduced movement. It commonly affects the knees, hips, shoulders, hands, and spine. Although arthritis is more common in older adults, it can affect people of all ages.

Physiotherapy plays an important role in managing arthritis by reducing pain, improving flexibility, strengthening muscles around the joints, and preventing further joint damage.

Common Symptoms

  • Joint pain
  • Joint stiffness, especially in the morning
  • Swelling around the joints
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Difficulty walking or climbing stairs
  • Pain during daily activities
  • Joint tenderness
  • Weakness around affected joints

Common Causes

  • Age-related joint degeneration
  • Osteoarthritis
  • Rheumatoid Arthritis
  • Previous joint injuries
  • Obesity
  • Genetic factors
  • Autoimmune disorders
  • Overuse of joints

Types of Arthritis

  • Osteoarthritis
  • Rheumatoid Arthritis
  • Psoriatic Arthritis
  • Gout
  • Ankylosing Spondylitis
  • Juvenile Arthritis

Tips to Manage Arthritis

  • Stay physically active.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Avoid prolonged sitting.
  • Exercise regularly.
  • Use proper footwear.
  • Protect your joints during activities.
  • Follow your physiotherapy exercise programme.
  • Consult a physiotherapist early when symptoms begin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can physiotherapy cure arthritis?

Physiotherapy cannot completely cure arthritis, but it can significantly reduce pain, improve mobility, strengthen muscles, and slow disease progression.

Is exercise safe for arthritis patients?

Yes. Properly guided exercises improve joint flexibility, reduce stiffness, and strengthen muscles supporting the joints.

How many physiotherapy sessions are needed?

The number of sessions depends on the severity of arthritis. Most patients notice improvement within 6–12 sessions.

Should I avoid walking if I have arthritis?

No. Walking in moderation is beneficial for most arthritis patients. Your physiotherapist will advise the right amount based on your condition.
