Heel Pain Treatment (Plantar Fasciitis) – Symptoms, Causes, Physiotherapy & Exercises

Learn how physiotherapy effectively treats heel pain and plantar fasciitis, helping you walk comfortably and return to your daily activities without pain.

What is Heel Pain?

Heel pain is one of the most common foot problems affecting people of all ages. The most frequent cause is Plantar Fasciitis, where the thick band of tissue supporting the arch of the foot becomes inflamed. Heel pain is usually worse during the first few steps in the morning or after prolonged sitting.

Early physiotherapy can reduce inflammation, improve flexibility, strengthen the foot muscles, and prevent long-term complications.

Common Symptoms

  • Sharp pain under the heel
  • Pain during the first steps in the morning
  • Pain after prolonged standing
  • Difficulty walking comfortably
  • Tenderness under the heel
  • Tight calf muscles
  • Foot stiffness
  • Pain after exercise

Common Causes

  • Plantar Fasciitis
  • Heel Spur
  • Flat Feet
  • High Foot Arch
  • Improper Footwear
  • Excess Body Weight
  • Running or Sports Activities
  • Standing for Long Hours
  • Tight Achilles Tendon
  • Poor Foot Mechanics

Risk Factors

  • People above 40 years
  • Teachers
  • Nurses
  • Factory Workers
  • Athletes and Runners
  • Overweight Individuals
  • People Wearing Unsupportive Footwear

Tips to Prevent Heel Pain

  • Wear comfortable supportive footwear.
  • Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors.
  • Stretch your calf muscles daily.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Avoid standing continuously for long periods.
  • Replace worn-out shoes regularly.
  • Warm up before sports activities.
  • Consult a physiotherapist if pain persists.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common cause of heel pain?

The most common cause is Plantar Fasciitis, which involves inflammation of the plantar fascia supporting the foot arch.

Can physiotherapy cure heel pain?

Yes. Most heel pain cases improve significantly with physiotherapy, stretching exercises, and proper footwear.

How long does recovery take?

Most patients notice improvement within 4 to 8 weeks, depending on the severity of the condition and adherence to exercises.

Should I stop walking if I have heel pain?

Not necessarily. Your physiotherapist will advise appropriate activity modification while continuing safe exercises to promote healing.
